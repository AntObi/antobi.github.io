<p class="page-intro">
I work where machine learning, atomistic simulation, and scientific software meet.
The projects below are the clearest public examples of that work; current company work is described
at a level that does not expose internal implementation details or results.
</p>

<div class="work-stack">
  <section class="work-item">
    <p class="eyebrow">01 · Scientific ML</p>
    <h2>Atomistic models and model infrastructure</h2>
    <p>
      My current work covers the lifecycle around materials ML models: data generation and preparation,
      training, evaluation, model interfaces, and integration into computational workflows. I am especially
      interested in machine-learning interatomic potentials and models that connect directly to atomistic
      simulation rather than living as isolated benchmarks.
    </p>
    <div class="tag-row">
      <span>ML potentials</span><span>atomistic simulation</span><span>model evaluation</span><span>HPC</span>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="work-item">
    <p class="eyebrow">02 · Open source</p>
    <h2>ElementEmbeddings</h2>
    <p>
      I developed ElementEmbeddings to make it easier to compare, analyse, and visualise representations
      of chemical elements and ionic species. The package grew directly out of research on whether learned
      and hand-designed representations recover useful notions of chemical similarity.
    </p>

  </section>

  <section class="work-item">
    <p class="eyebrow">03 · Materials informatics</p>
    <h2>SMACT and chemically informed screening</h2>
    <p>
      I contribute to SMACT, an open-source toolkit for fast chemistry-aware screening in materials design.
      Recent work around SMACT has focused on modernising the software and on treating chemical validity
      as a configurable prior for screening and generative materials workflows.
    </p>
    <div class="card-links">
      <a href="https://github.com/WMD-group/SMACT">GitHub</a>
      <a href="https://zenodo.org/records/22941452">SMACT v4</a>
      <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17910">2026 preprint</a>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="work-item">
    <p class="eyebrow">04 · Generative materials design</p>
    <h2>Mapping and navigating crystal chemical space</h2>
    <p>
      I have collaborated on work that maps large inorganic composition spaces using learned chemical
      representations and on text-guided generative models for crystal structures. The underlying question
      is the same one that motivates much of my work: how do we encode enough chemistry and physics to make
      large search spaces tractable without reducing the problem to brittle hand-written rules?
    </p>
    <div class="card-links">
      <a href="https://doi.org/10.1039/D4FD00063C">Chemical-space mapping</a>
      <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59636-y">Text-guided generation</a>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="work-item">
    <p class="eyebrow">05 · Computational materials science</p>
    <h2>High-throughput first-principles materials screening</h2>
    <p>
      My PhD focused on rapid computational screening for energy-storage materials. It combined
      high-throughput density-functional-theory calculations, thermodynamic reasoning, chemical
      screening, and data-driven analysis to narrow large candidate spaces.
    </p>

      <a href="https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/entities/publication/54dc546d-71cc-42f1-856c-83991809f017">PhD thesis</a>
