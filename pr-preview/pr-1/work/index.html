    Work

    I work where machine learning, atomistic simulation, and scientific software meet. The projects below are the clearest public examples of that work; current company work is described at a level that does not expose internal implementation details or results.

    01 · Scientific ML

    Atomistic models and model infrastructure

    My current work covers the lifecycle around materials ML models: data generation and preparation, training, evaluation, model interfaces, and integration into computational workflows. I am especially interested in machine-learning interatomic potentials and models that connect directly to atomistic simulation rather than living as isolated benchmarks.

    ML potentialsatomistic simulationmodel evaluationHPC

    02 · Open source

    ElementEmbeddings

    I developed ElementEmbeddings to make it easier to compare, analyse, and visualise representations of chemical elements and ionic species. The package grew directly out of research on whether learned and hand-designed representations recover useful notions of chemical similarity.

    GitHub Zenodo Digital Discovery APL Machine Learning

    03 · Materials informatics

    SMACT and chemically informed screening

    I contribute to SMACT, an open-source toolkit for fast chemistry-aware screening in materials design. Recent work around SMACT has focused on modernising the software and on treating chemical validity as a configurable prior for screening and generative materials workflows.

    GitHub SMACT v4 2026 preprint

    04 · Generative materials design

    Mapping and navigating crystal chemical space

    I have collaborated on work that maps large inorganic composition spaces using learned chemical representations and on text-guided generative models for crystal structures. The underlying question is the same one that motivates much of my work: how do we encode enough chemistry and physics to make large search spaces tractable without reducing the problem to brittle hand-written rules?

    Chemical-space mapping Text-guided generation

    05 · Computational materials science

    High-throughput first-principles materials screening

    My PhD focused on rapid computational screening for energy-storage materials. It combined high-throughput density-functional-theory calculations, thermodynamic reasoning, chemical screening, and data-driven analysis to narrow large candidate spaces.
